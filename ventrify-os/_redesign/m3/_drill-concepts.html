Drilling into a document

Four working concepts for what happens when you open a research workstream. Each frame below is live — click the documents, click the sections, scroll the text and watch the navigation keep up.

What is wrong today, measured. Opening a document sets the shell grid to 80px 360px 928px 0 72px. The parent screen is not hidden, it is crushed to 360px — the hero headline truncates mid-word and the thesis becomes a ribbon of two-word lines that nobody reads. It is a vestige, not context. Then the document itself opens with an “On this page” card that costs about 250px above the fold and repeats the “7 sections” already printed in the meta line directly above it — so the first thing you see on opening a document is a list of the things you could read instead of the document.

All four kill both. They differ in where navigation lives, and that is the real decision: a permanent rail costs horizontal space on every screen but makes moving between documents free; an on-demand index costs a click but gives the text the full width. The right answer depends on whether an operator reads one document or sweeps seven.
