Two accents, measured

Five candidate pairs for VerdictScore and VentureStack. Each one is shown doing the accent's real jobs — the active tab, eyebrow labels, the score dial, the valuation band, the highlighted table row, a filled button — rather than as swatches, because an accent that looks good as a square can still fail as 11px text.

Only five tokens change per identity — --brand-accent (the saturated fill), --brand-accent-2 (the light step, which is the TEXT colour), --brand-tint, --text-on-accent and --photo-grade. Everything else — surfaces, type, spacing, status colours — is shared, which is why all three products can look like one family. So this choice is genuinely only about these two hues.

⚠ Worth knowing before you choose: both current accents have already bent a status colour. assets/brand.css says it outright — “OK is LIME, not emerald, and that is deliberate. VentureStack's accent is #10C981; an emerald success colour sat 16 units from it, which is the same colour to the eye.” And --info is defined as var(--v-blue-400), which is VerdictScore's accent. So today a green bar could mean “good” or “VentureStack”, and a blue one could mean “info” or “VerdictScore”. Moving off blue and green frees both: success could go back to a true emerald instead of a compromise lime, and info could stop impersonating a brand. That is a real gain on top of the aesthetics, and it is the strongest argument for changing at all.
