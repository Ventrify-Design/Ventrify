01 — The Challenge

Healthcare that feels human

Healthcare access remains deeply uneven — not because care doesn’t exist, but because the systems designed to deliver it are fragmented, opaque, and intimidating to navigate.

The founder had identified a clear gap between what patients needed — continuity, clarity, and genuine personalisation — and what existing digital health tools delivered.

The challenge was to build something that felt trustworthy enough for a medical context, yet approachable enough that people would actually use it every day.

02 — Phase 1, Phase 2 · The Data Room

Three Provocation Cards.
One product locked at Gate 2.

Phase 1 Discover ran first — the Research Hub L3 mapped anxiety points, drop-off moments, and where existing apps failed to earn long-term engagement. research-curator distilled the findings into Provocation Cards. The founder resolved them in the portal, the Vision Hub rebuilt against the decisions, and the product locked around three principles before Gate 2 closed.

Card 01

Intelligent simplicity

AI-surfaced insights that felt helpful, not clinical. Data framed as action, not noise.

Card 02

Seamless access

Telemedicine built into the flow rather than bolted on. Booking to consult in three taps.

Card 03

Proactive guidance

Preventive recommendations that anticipated needs before they became problems.

Phase 3 Design ran in parallel with the Financials Hub (Phase 2.5). The ux-designer agent produced three audit rounds across the AI health tracking interface, calibrating data legibility against clinical credibility. information-architect mapped 47 screens to the founder-resolved cards.
